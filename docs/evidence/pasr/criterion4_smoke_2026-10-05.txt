== Isolation and fix attempt (5 October, 03:45 to 03:57; control run frt 32x6, Mac) ==
Each test below used a temporary environment switch or print in core/, reverted afterwards,
except the axis-row clamp, which is kept (core/flow.cpp, radialProfiles).

Test A. k and omega face gradient replaced by the two-point difference along the line of centres
(no off-line part), unchanged otherwise. Result: stops at 0.135982 ms, same cell (i 18, j 0),
rho*omega 1.39e-6 at the step start, stage -1.82e-6 at dt 4.42e-12: drain about 7e5 per second,
the same rate as before at a 30 times smaller rho*omega. So the off-line diffusion is not the
cause of this stop.

Test B. k and omega diffusion switched off. Result: stops earlier, at 0.092520 ms, in a different
cell (wall row, i 16 j 5), where omega decays to 1e-117 and then NaN without the wall's omega
supply. Inconclusive for the first stop.

Mechanism of the first stop (inferred from the code and the measured drain): on the axis row,
mass fractions, k and omega are reconstructed with an r^2 curvature limited only by minmod of the
two neighbour differences. Nothing bounds it by the cell value. With omega 1.9e-3 in the axis cell
and 4.5e3 and 2.8e4 in the next two rings, the face value at the first face off the axis is of the
order of the neighbours, and the outward radial mass flux past the throat carries that out of a
cell that holds almost nothing, at any step size. The wall row already bounds its one-sided slope
so both faces stay within half the cell value; the axis row did not.

Fix attempt 1 (kept): the axis-row curvature is clamped so the profile stays within half the cell
value of it at the first face and on the axis (reach = max(M_face - M_cell, M_cell), as the wall
row's reach). Result: the run passes 0.136 ms, lights at 0.2060 ms (largest Y_H2O 0.616; chamber
volume with s > 0.01: 0.0197), and stops at 0.345931 ms. Second stop: cell (i 26, j 1) in the
divergent nozzle, rho*omega 2.0e-34 at the step start (omega about 1.6e-32 1/s, k about 700
m^2/s^2), stage -6.1e-8 at dt 1.67e-11: drain about 3.6e3 per second whatever the cell holds.

Test C (on top of the clamp). Two-point k and omega diffusion as in test A. Result: reaches the
0.5 ms end. (The harness then extended the run, because its extension rule did not yet require
an 8 ms run; fixed in the harness.) It stops at 0.523999 ms: cells (i 26, j 4) and (i 27, j 3) in
the divergent nozzle, rho*omega exactly 0 after the source half step, rho*k 3e-109 and 3e-116.
So the second stop is the off-line part of the k and omega diffusion (inferred: removing it
removes the stop), and there is a third: omega decays to underflow in the nozzle expansion.

Third mechanism (inferred, not isolated): in the expanding nozzle the SST sources take omega down
with no floor. Two candidate sinks: the dilatation part of the production, -(2/3) rho k div u,
which enters omega's equation as about -(2/3) gamma rho omega div u (a decay at a rate of order
the expansion rate, 1e5 to 1e6 1/s here); and the cross-diffusion term 2 (1 - F1) rho sigma_w2 /
omega grad k . grad omega, a sink when grad k . grad omega < 0, whose relative rate grows as
omega falls. The MPRK source keeps omega positive in exact arithmetic, not below the double range. The engine has ambient values (Spalart-Rumsey) for the fill but not the sustaining terms
of SST-2003sust (NASA TMR), which hold k and omega at those values in free stream.

Stopped here (three distinct faults in a row). Candidates for the next session:
 2. Limit the off-line part of the k and omega face gradient so a face's flux keeps the sign of
    the two-point difference (OpenFOAM's limited Laplacian; monotone under the existing step limit).
 3. Either the SST-2003sust sustaining terms (a published model variant, a modelling choice to be
    recorded) or a floor on omega at the ambient value; the first is preferred.

2.8e-100 (26,4), 8.6e-100 (27,5) 1/s, with k 20 to 560 m^2/s^2 in the same cells.

Mechanism of the collapse (from the code, then tested). Omega's production is gamma rho P / mu_t,
P = mu_t (S^2 - 2/3 div^2) - 2/3 rho k div. Where the SST limiter is active (a1 omega < S F2),
rho k / mu_t = S F2 / a1 instead of omega, so the production is
  gamma rho [(S^2 - 2/3 div^2) - (2/3) div S F2 / a1],
which in a strong expansion (div > 0 large against the deviatoric strain) is negative and does
not depend on omega. MPRK22 books a negative production as destruction, coefficient |P_w| / omega,
so each substep maps omega to about omega^2 / (dt |P_w|): positive, but collapsing faster than
exponentially, which matches the 1e-100 to 1e-138 values. Once omega is that small, any drain
(the off-line diffusion of fault 2, or round-off) is larger than the cell holds.

Isolation test 1 (04:12; nodil_stdout.txt): limiter on, and the dilatation part removed from
omega's production only (temporary; the k equation unchanged). Result: reaches the 0.5 ms end,
positivity ok at all 251 samples, mass 5.3e-14, energy 3.1e-15; fails only "settled" (c), as any
0.5 ms run must. Wall 17 s.
Isolation test 2 (04:14; nolim_nodil_stdout.txt): the same with the limiter off (the committed
diffusion). Result: the same, reaches 0.5 ms with positivity ok at all samples.
So faults 2 and 3 are one fault (measured: removing the omega-independent sink removes both
stops; the diffusion limiter alone does not). The limiter is not kept.

NASA TMR (sst.html, now at https://tmbwg.github.io/turbmodels/sst.html, read 5 October): base
SST-2003 uses P = tau_ij du_i/dx_j with the -2/3 rho k delta_ij term, and omega's production
gamma / nu_t P. The page lists approximations: (SSTm) ignores 2/3 rho k in tau_ij and uses
P = mu_t S^2 ("most common"); (SSTs) keeps it in tau_ij and uses P = mu_t S^2; (SSTe) ignores it
and keeps the exact P. With P = mu_t S^2 omega's production cannot go negative.

Fix kept (04:16; core/transport.cpp, TECHNICAL_PLAN step 7). In omega's production only, the
dilatation part -(2/3) (rho k / mu_t) div uses omega for rho k / mu_t:
  production = gamma min(S^2 - 2/3 div^2 - (2/3) omega div, 10 beta* omega rho k / mu_t).
Where the limiter is inactive (a1 omega >= S F2) this is algebraically the exact form. Where it is
active, the dilatation part becomes a decay at a rate of at most (2/3) gamma div, not a sink that does
not scale with omega. The k equation keeps the exact P. Chosen over the SSTs form (P = mu_t S^2 in
both equations) because SSTs also removes the real -(2/3) rho k div from k's budget, which is
physical work in a compressible flow. A CRUCIBLE choice, flagged for Ben.
